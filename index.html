Inertia Lock Weigh Cell

Everything sits on one weldment table: a FlexiBowl, a SCARA robot, and a load cell on its own floor post that rises through a cutout in the table without touching it. In one move, the SCARA pushes the last part off the scale into a chute and sets the new part down. A diverter that rests on REJECT routes each part to the GOOD or REJECT bin.

Why it works

  • The scale doesn't touch the table. The load cell sits on a floor post with a rubber isolation pad, through a 150 mm cutout in the tabletop. Robot and FlexiBowl vibration stays in the weldment.
  • Place, don't slide. The part is released onto the pan, so the reading has no friction or side load. Rails along the front and back of the pan keep the runner from hanging over the edge.
  • Sweep and place in one move. A paddle mounted to the side of the gripper pushes the weighed part off the pan into the chute. The paddle ends up past the pan edge, so the gripper is already over the pan center and sets the new part down. No tipping tray or air blow-off needed.
  • Each part sits on the scale until the next cycle. That gives the reading up to 3.4 s to settle while the robot is away.
  • Gripper on the runner stub. A SCARA picks straight down, which suits parts lying flat on the backlit bowl.

Fail-safe and guarding

  • Diverter rests on REJECT. It only moves to GOOD for a part that has a valid pass result. Any power loss, fault or timeout sends parts to REJECT.
  • GOOD arrival is confirmed. A through-beam sensor in the GOOD chute must see each part, or the cell stops.
  • Locked REJECT bin. The lid has a slot for parts to fall in, and only QA can unlock it.
  • Bad feed goes to a rework tote. Loose runners, clusters and flash go through the FlexiBowl purge.
  • Polycarbonate enclosure on the table with an interlocked door. No floor fence, so the cell can be moved with a forklift or casters.
  • Per-part log on the HMI (time, weight, pass/fail, lot), plus a known-good and a known-short-shot challenge part run every shift.

To confirm

  • Runner weight range, and a set of good parts and known short shots for a weighing study. The 18.55 g limit shown here is only an example.
  • Part dimensions with runner, to size the pan, rails and gripper jaws.
  • Floor space and bin positions. The Hi-Boy sits outside the table on the right.
  • Whether 3230/3231 (about 145 g) are in scope. If so, size the load cell and FlexiBowl for both parts.

The 3D model and timing are concept-level. Cycle time still needs to be checked in a robot simulation.
